01 · The learning model

A connector is a signal, not decoration.

Spanish and English organize the same core relationships between ideas — cause, contrast, addition, emphasis. English simply requires a dedicated word to mark them out loud. Skip the word, and the relationship still exists in your head; the reader just has to guess it.

Ideas create the content. Connectors create the flow.

A reader follows an argument through the relationships between sentences, not through any single sentence alone. "And" and "but" can express almost any relationship, badly. The right connector expresses one relationship precisely — first identify what two sentences are doing to each other, then choose the word that names it.

Important: a connector that feels too formal for speech is often exactly right for writing. Written English rewards more explicit signposting than conversation does — reaching for "consequently" in an essay is not overkill.
02 · The anatomy of a description

Know what you are building.

A description moves through four stages: gather raw material, draft without editing, revise with structure in mind, then proofread. Connectors do their most important work in stage three, at the paragraph's hinge points.

Topic sentence

States the subject and the angle in one clear sentence — the reader's first orientation.

Super connector

Placed near the middle of the paragraph, it signals the turn: a cause, a contrast, an example, an added point.

Supporting detail

Concrete, specific evidence — physical traits, actions, or examples that make the description real.

Closing sentence

Restates the point with a conclusion or emphasis connector so the paragraph feels finished, not cut off.

03 · Confidence is part of technique

Break the flat-paragraph loop.

Without a working set of connectors, writers default to the two or three they already automated. The result reads flat even when the ideas are strong — and the writer usually blames their English, not their toolkit.

01

Default habit

"And" and "but" cover every relationship because they're the only ones on autopilot.

02

Flat rhythm

Every sentence follows the same short, choppy pattern with no signaled hierarchy.

03

Reader effort

The reader has to reconstruct relationships between ideas that were never marked.

04

Self-doubt

The writer assumes the problem is their English level, not a missing toolkit.

05

Avoidance

New connectors go untried because a mistake feels visible on the page, unlike a mispronounced sound that disappears in the air.

Reset the interpretation: "My paragraph is missing signposts, not evidence of low proficiency." Practice one category at a time, listen to the model sentences, swap in your own ideas, and read the result aloud to hear where the flow breaks.

05 · Sentence builder & listening

Hear. Swap. Repeat.

Pick a connector from the atlas and one of its category's two model sentences. Listen once, notice how the connector shifts the sentence's logic, then swap in your own idea using the same word. A few connectors — the ones that need a noun phrase or a verb right after them, like "owing to" or "leads to" — sit out of the builder to keep every sentence grammatically clean; tap them in the atlas above to hear them instead.

BUILT SENTENCE

Three-pass method: 1) listen without speaking; 2) repeat the built sentence just behind the slow model; 3) write your own version, keeping the same connector in the same position.
