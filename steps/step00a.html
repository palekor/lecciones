01 · The governing idea

Choose by function, not translation.

Spanish often allows the subject to disappear and expresses possession differently. English usually requires an explicit subject and uses position to reveal which pronoun form belongs there.

First identify the job. Then choose the form.

01 · DOERSubjectPerforms the verb. Usually comes before it.
02 · RECEIVERObjectReceives the action or follows a preposition.
03 · OWNER + THINGPossessive adjectiveDescribes a noun, so the noun must follow.
04 · OWNER ALONEPossessive pronounReplaces the owner + noun phrase. No noun follows.
05 · RETURNReflexiveThe subject and object are the same, or it adds emphasis.
She gave me her book. The red one is hers. She made it herself.

03 · Five grammatical jobs

Position reveals meaning.

Select a form to see its sentence position, purpose, and the contrast that makes it different from the other four.

04 · Contrasts that matter

Similar forms, different logic.

Most mistakes disappear when you ask one small question: “Is a noun coming next?” Then check whether the form is doing or receiving the action.

My vs. mine

✓

This is my house.“My” describes the noun “house.”

✓

This house is mine.“Mine” replaces “my house.”

×

This is mine house.A possessive pronoun cannot describe a following noun.

Her vs. hers

✓

I called her.Object: she receives the action.

✓

Her recipe is excellent.Possessive adjective + noun.

✓

The recipe is hers.Possessive pronoun; no noun follows.

Its vs. it’s

✓

The dog moved its tail.“Its” shows possession. No apostrophe.

✓

It’s happy.“It’s” contracts “it is” or “it has.”

×

The dog moved it’s tail.Expand it: “it is tail” proves this is incorrect.

Subject vs. object

✓

She is with us.She = subject; us follows the preposition “with.”

✓

I am with her.I performs “am”; her follows “with.”

×

Her is with we.Object forms cannot fill these subject/object positions.

05 · Every person in context

Follow each form through a story.

Each paragraph keeps one person active while changing grammatical jobs. Read the colored pronouns and name their job before continuing.

I

I · me · my · mine

I love reading books. My friends gave me this one for my birthday. It is my favorite book. No one can take it from me. It is mine.

You

You · you · your · yours

You are very smart. Your teacher praised you for your work. You should be proud of yourself. Those achievements are yours.

He

He · him · his · his

He is a good singer. I heard him sing yesterday. His voice is amazing. He should share his talent with the world. Those songs are his.

She

She · her · her · hers

She is a great cook. She invited me to her house. Her food was delicious. She made everything by herself. The recipes are hers.

It

It · it · its · itself

It is a cute dog. It followed me home. Its fur is soft and fluffy. I like to play with it. The dog can entertain itself with its toys.

We

We · us · our · ours

We are a team. We support each other. Our coach trained us well. We prepared ourselves. The final victory is ours.

You

You plural · you · your · yours

You are my friends. You helped me a lot. Your kindness is appreciated. You deserve the best. This celebration is yours; enjoy yourselves.

They

They · them · their · theirs

They are my neighbors. They moved in last week. Their house is beautiful. I met them yesterday. They decorated it themselves. The style is theirs.

06 · Advanced clarity

Three details learners miss.

These points complete the system and prevent rules from becoming misleading shortcuts.

Singular “they”

Use they / them / their / theirs / themselves for a person whose gender is unknown, irrelevant, or nonbinary: “Someone left their phone. I hope they find it.”

Reflexive has two uses

Return: “She taught herself.” Emphasis: “She made it herself.” Do not use a reflexive simply to sound formal: say “Contact me,” not “Contact myself.”

“It” rarely has a possessive pronoun

English uses its + noun but normally avoids an independent equivalent of “mine.” Rephrase: “Each animal has its own territory,” not “The territory is its.”
