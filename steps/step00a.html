Exposure · 01 · System logic

Why this lesson comes first.

In Spanish, the verb carries the person. In English, the pronoun does. Before verbs, chunks or questions, every English sentence asks you for one decision: who? Get it right, and the rest of the sentence organizes itself.

Spanish · español

The person lives inside the verb.

The ending tells you who. That is why Spanish can drop the subject.

yotrabajo
tútrabajas
él / ellatrabaja
nosotrostrabajamos
ellostrabajan
Five endings. “Trabajo en casa” is a complete sentence — no subject needed.
English

The person lives in the pronoun.

The verb barely changes, so the pronoun must appear — always.

II work
youYou work
he / she / itShe works
weWe work
theyThey work
Almost one form. Only he / she / it adds -s. Remove the pronoun and nobody knows who works.
Law 01

Never drop the subject.

Even when Spanish has nothing to translate, English needs a word in the subject position.

Es importante.
Is important. → It is important.
Law 02

The pronoun steers the verb.

Choose the pronoun first. The verb agrees with it: am / is / are, work / works.

Agreement
I am · She is · They are
Law 03

Choose by job, not translation.

One Spanish word can open five English doors. The owner and the job decide — never the dictionary.

Exposure · 02 · The governing idea

Choose by function, not translation.

Spanish often lets the subject disappear and expresses possession differently. English requires an explicit subject and uses position to reveal which form belongs there. Five jobs. Five questions.

First identify the job. Then choose the form.

01 · DOERSubjectPerforms the verb. Usually comes before it.Who does it?
02 · RECEIVERObjectReceives the action or follows a preposition.Who receives it?
03 · OWNER + THINGPossessive adjectiveDescribes a noun, so the noun must follow.Is a noun next?
04 · OWNER ALONEPossessive pronounReplaces owner + noun. No noun follows.Does it stand alone?
05 · RETURNReflexiveSubject and object are the same — or it adds emphasis.Does it come back?
She gave me her book. The red one is hers. She made it herself.

Integration · 06 · Interference map

Eight traps Spanish sets for you.

Most pronoun errors are not English mistakes. They are Spanish logic leaking into English. Name the leak and it stops. Hear every correct version — your ear needs the right model, not the wrong one.

Trap 01 · “su”

One Spanish word, five owners.

×

My mother loves his garden.

✓

My mother loves her garden.

“Su” hides the owner. English shows it: his (él), her (ella), its (cosa), their (ellos), your (usted). Look at the owner, never at the thing.

Trap 02 · dropped subject

English never leaves the seat empty.

×

Is important. / Works in Bogotá.

✓

It is important. She works in Bogotá.

Weather, time, opinions and distances also need it: It is raining. It is late. It is far.

Trap 03 · “me and…”

Remove the other person.

×

Me and Ana study together.

✓

Ana and I study together. She called Ana and me.

Delete “Ana and”: I study ✓ · She called me ✓. The test never fails. Courtesy puts the other person first.

Trap 04 · my vs. mine

Is a noun coming next?

×

This is mine car.

✓

This is my car. This car is mine.

Noun next → my, your, her, our, their. Nothing next → mine, yours, hers, ours, theirs.

Trap 05 · its vs. it’s

The apostrophe is not possession here.

×

The dog moved it’s tail.

✓

The dog moved its tail. It’s happy.

Expand it: “it is tail” is impossible. It’s = it is / it has. Its = possession.

Trap 06 · body parts

English names the owner of the body.

×

I wash the hands. She broke the arm.

✓

I wash my hands. She broke her arm.

Spanish uses the article (“me lavo las manos”). English uses the possessive adjective.

Trap 07 · lo / la / le

Object pronouns move after the verb.

×

Lo vi → I him saw.

✓

I saw him. I bought it. I called her.

Spanish puts the object before the verb. English puts it after: subject → verb → object. “Lo” can be him or it — decide by the person or thing.

Trap 08 · fake formality

“Myself” does not sound more polite.

×

Please contact myself for details.

✓

Please contact me for details.

Use a reflexive only when the action returns to the doer (I taught myself) or for emphasis (I did it myself).

Integration · 08 · Advanced clarity

Four details advanced speakers own.

These points complete the system and stop the rules from becoming misleading shortcuts.

Singular “they”

Use they / them / their / theirs / themselves for one person whose gender is unknown, irrelevant or nonbinary: “Someone left their phone. I hope they find it.”

Reflexive has two uses

Return: “She taught herself.” Emphasis: “She made it herself.” By + reflexive means alone: “I live by myself.”

“It” has no “mine”

English uses its + noun but avoids an independent form. Rephrase: “Each animal has its own territory,” not “The territory is its.”

After “than” and “as”

Conversation: “She is taller than me.” Formal or with a verb: “She is taller than I am.” Both are correct; “than I” alone sounds stiff.
