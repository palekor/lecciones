01 · The decisive test

Progressive has two verb parts.

The pronoun itself is not conjugated. The verb BE changes to agree with the subject and carry the tense; the main verb receives ‑ing.

Progressive = conjugated BE + main verb ending in ‑ing.

Both pieces belong to one verb phrase. Without the correct form of BE, an ‑ing word does not create a progressive tense.

PART 01 · SUBJECTsheWho performs or experiences the action?
+
PART 02 · FINITE BEisAgrees with she and marks present time.
+
PART 03 · MAIN VERBplayingPlay + ‑ing presents the activity unfolding.
She is playing now = progressive.   She likes playing = not progressive.
02 · Implications of use

Same action, different viewpoint.

Mode changes what the listener understands. Simple looks at an action as a fact, habit, schedule, state, or complete unit. Progressive looks inside a developing activity.

SIMPLE VIEWPOINT

She plays tennis.

This identifies tennis as her routine, skill, or usual activity. It does not mean she is on the court now.

SHE + PLAYS
  • habit: every Saturday
  • fact: she knows how to play
  • schedule: class starts at nine
  • state: she likes tennis
PROGRESSIVE VIEWPOINT

She is playing tennis.

This places us inside the activity around now or another specified reference moment. It may be temporary.

SHE + IS + PLAYING
  • happening now
  • temporary current situation
  • changing development
  • arranged future context

State verbs usually stay simple.

Verbs such as know, understand, believe, want, need, like, love, and possession have normally describe states rather than unfolding activities: I know the answer, not I am knowing the answer. A verb may become progressive when its meaning becomes active: They have a car (possession) but They are having lunch (activity).

03 · Tense × mode

Six different messages.

Tense answers “when?” Mode answers “how do I view the action?” Combine past, present, or future with simple or progressive deliberately.

Past
Past simpleShe played yesterday.

Finished event; regular verbs use ‑ed, irregular verbs change form.

Past progressiveShe was playing at 7.

The activity was unfolding around a past moment.

Present
Present simpleShe plays every Saturday.

Routine or stable fact; he/she/it normally adds ‑s or ‑es.

Present progressiveShe is playing now.

BE agrees with the subject; the main verb ends in ‑ing.

Future
Future simpleShe will play tomorrow.

Prediction, decision, promise, or future fact.

Future progressiveShe will be playing at 7.

The activity will be unfolding around a future moment.

05 · The ‑ing decoder

Not every ‑ing word is progressive.

The spelling is not enough. Find the finite verb and ask what job the ‑ing form performs. Only BE + verb‑ing functioning as the verb phrase creates progressive aspect.

He is playing soccer.

is + playing forms one present-progressive verb phrase. The activity is in progress.

COMPARE

He likes playing soccer.

likes is the finite present-simple verb. Playing soccer names the activity he likes.

Progressive verb phrase

Conjugated BE + ‑ing shows an activity unfolding.

They are studying now.

Gerund as activity/noun

The ‑ing form names an activity after verbs such as like, love, enjoy, or hate.

She likes reading.

Gerund after a preposition

After at, before, after, without, and many other prepositions, use an ‑ing form.

He is good at drawing.

Modifier or description

An ‑ing form may describe a noun or what produces a feeling.

It is an interesting lesson.
Proof test: In “He likes playing,” replacing playing with a noun still works: “He likes soccer.” Therefore, playing is the activity he likes—not the progressive verb. In “He is playing,” removing playing destroys the action.

07 · Questions and negatives

Different auxiliaries, different systems.

Simple present and past normally use DO. Progressive forms already contain BE, so BE moves before the subject in questions and receives not in negatives.

Simple mode

Does she play? → She does not play.Did she play? → She did not play.Will she play? → She will not play.

After do, does, or did, use the base verb: “Did she play?” not “Did she played?”

Correction: ordinary past affirmation is “He loved her.” “He did love her” is possible, but did adds special emphasis.

Progressive mode

Is she playing? → She is not playing.Was she playing? → She was not playing.Will she be playing? → She will not be playing.

Do not add DO to a progressive question. The conjugated BE is already the auxiliary: “Is she working?” not “Does she is working?”

Two-part check: every progressive example retains BE and the main verb in ‑ing.
