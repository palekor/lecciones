Exposure · 01 · System logic

Every verb answers two questions.

In Step 00a you chose who. Now the verb must answer when — past, present, future — and how you look at the action: the complete photo or the unfolding video. Spanish often lets one form do both jobs. English makes you choose the camera.

Simple mode · the photo

The whole action, from outside.

A fact, a habit, a schedule, a finished event, a prediction.

She works in a bank.stable fact
She worked late yesterday.finished event
She will work tomorrow.decision · prediction
Progressive mode · the video

Inside the action, while it moves.

Happening around a reference moment — often temporary.

She is working right now.in progress now
She was working at 7 last night.in progress at a past moment
She will be working at 7 tomorrow.in progress at a future moment
Law 01

Progressive always has two parts.

BE carries person and time. The main verb carries the action with ‑ing. Remove either part and the video breaks.

Two parts
She playing. → She is playing.
Law 02

The pronoun steers the verb.

Straight from 00a: the subject chooses am / is / are, was / were — and the ‑s of he, she, it.

Agreement
I am · she is · they were · he works
Law 03

Choose the camera, not the translation.

“¿Qué haces?” has two English answers. One asks about your life; the other asks about this moment.

¿Qué haces?
What do you do? · What are you doing?

Exposure · 02 · The decisive test

Progressive has two verb parts.

The pronoun never changes form here. BE changes to agree with the subject and to carry time; the main verb receives ‑ing and never changes again.

Progressive = conjugated BE + main verb ending in ‑ing.

Both pieces belong to one verb phrase. Without the correct form of BE, an ‑ing word does not create a progressive tense.

Perception · 03 · Implications of use

Same action, different viewpoint.

Mode changes what the listener understands. Simple looks at the action as a unit. Progressive looks inside a developing activity — and usually implies it is temporary.

SIMPLE VIEWPOINT

She plays tennis.

Tennis is her routine, skill or usual activity. It does not mean she is on the court now.

  • HabitShe plays every Saturday.
  • FactWater boils at 100°C.
  • ScheduleClass starts at nine.
  • StateShe likes tennis.
PROGRESSIVE VIEWPOINT

She is playing tennis.

We are inside the activity around now — or around another moment we name. It may be temporary.

  • Happening nowLook! She is serving.
  • TemporaryShe is living with her aunt this month.
  • ChangingHer English is getting better.
  • Arranged futureShe is meeting the coach on Friday.

State verbs usually stay simple.

They describe states, not unfolding activities: I know the answer — never “I am knowing”. A verb can go progressive when its meaning becomes an activity.

State · possessionThey have a car.
Activity · eatingThey are having lunch.

Perception · 06 · The ‑ing decoder

Not every ‑ing word is progressive.

Spelling is not enough. Find the conjugated verb, then ask what job the ‑ing form does. Only BE + verb‑ing working as the verb phrase creates the progressive.

He is playing soccer.

is + playing = one present-progressive verb phrase. The activity is in progress.

COMPARE

He likes playing soccer.

likes is the conjugated verb. playing soccer names the activity he likes.

Progressive

Conjugated BE + ‑ing: an activity unfolding.

They are studying.

Activity name

After like, love, enjoy, hate, or as a subject.

She enjoys reading.

After a preposition

at, before, after, without, about… + ‑ing.

He's good at drawing.

Description

An ‑ing adjective describes a noun.

An interesting lesson.
Proof test: in “He likes playing,” swap playing for a noun: “He likes soccer” still works — so playing is the thing he likes, not the verb. In “He is playing,” remove playing and the action disappears.

Integration · 09 · Interference map

Eight traps Spanish sets for you.

These are not random mistakes. Each one is Spanish logic running inside English. Name the leak and it stops — and hear the correct model, never the wrong one.

Trap 01 · “I am work”

Two systems in one sentence.

×

I am work every day.

✓

I work every day. I am working now.

“Soy / estoy” tempts you to add BE. Simple uses the verb alone. BE appears only with ‑ing (or before nouns and adjectives).

Trap 02 · missing ‑s

He, she, it need the ‑s.

×

She work in a bank.

✓

She works in a bank. He studies at night.

Present simple only. The ‑s is the one place English verbs still show the person — say it, don’t swallow it.

Trap 03 · “¿Qué haces?”

Job or this moment?

×

(on the phone) What do you do now?

✓

What are you doing? — now. What do you do? — your job.

Spanish uses one present for both. English separates them: simple = your life, progressive = this moment.

Trap 04 · state verbs

Knowing is not an activity.

×

I am knowing the answer. I am needing help.

✓

I know the answer. I need help.

Know, want, need, understand, believe, belong stay simple — even right now.

Trap 05 · DO vs. BE

One auxiliary per question.

×

Do you are working? Does she is coming?

✓

Are you working? Is she coming?

Progressive already has BE — it moves to the front. DO belongs to the simple system.

Trap 06 · double past

DID already carries the past.

×

Did she played? I didn't went.

✓

Did she play? I didn't go.

The past lives in did. The main verb returns to its base form.

Trap 07 · imperfecto

“Cocinaba cuando llegué.”

×

Ella cocinaba → She cooked when I arrived.

✓

She was cooking when I arrived.

“She cooked when I arrived” means she started after you came. An action already in progress → past progressive.

Trap 08 · “Te llamo mañana”

Promises take WILL.

×

I call you tomorrow.

✓

I'll call you tomorrow.

Spanish uses the present for promises and offers. English uses will for decisions made now.

Integration · 10 · Questions and negatives

Two auxiliaries, two systems.

Simple present and past borrow DO. Progressive already contains BE, so BE moves before the subject for questions and takes not for negatives. Future uses WILL in both.

Simple mode

Auxiliary: do · does · did · will
After do, does, did the main verb is in its base form: “Did she play?” — not “Did she played?”

Progressive mode

Auxiliary: am · is · are · was · were · will be
Never add DO: “Is she working?” — not “Does she is working?” Both parts stay: BE + ‑ing.
