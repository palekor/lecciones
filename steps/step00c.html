Exposure · 01 · System logic

Stop memorizing verbs. Run the engine.

Weak method: “eat = comer.” Strong method: subject + auxiliary + verb form + complement + time. Step 00a gave you the subject. Step 00b gave you the camera. Now you get the whole machine — and the one rule that controls it.

Every English verb phrase has five slots.

Not every slot is always full — but the order never changes. The auxiliary is the steering wheel: it carries time, agreement, questions and negation.

Law 01 · the chain rule

Each auxiliary chooses the next form.

do / modals → base · be → ‑ing (or participle) · have → participle. Learn four arrows, unlock twelve tenses.

has → been → working
Law 02 · the first auxiliary

Only the first auxiliary moves.

For a question it jumps before the subject. For a negative it takes not. Everything after it stays still.

Has she been working? · She hasn't.
Law 03 · no auxiliary?

Borrow DO.

Present and past simple have no auxiliary in the positive — so questions and negatives borrow do / does / did + base verb.

She works. → Does she work?

Exposure · 02 · The working pieces

Every verb has five forms.

When a student says “I am work” or “She doesn’t works,” the problem is not vocabulary — it is form control. Pick a verb: each form has one job and one trigger. Tap any form to hear it.

Infinitive = to + base: purpose and many patterns — “I want to learn.” Never after modals: “can learn”, not “can to learn”.
Perception · 03 · The chain rule

Four arrows run the whole system.

This is the hidden logic behind every tense, question and passive in English. Read each chain left to right: every auxiliary decides the form of the word that comes next.

Rule 1do · does · did→ BASE
Rule 2will · can · should · must…→ BASE
Rule 3am · is · are · was · were · be · been→ ‑ING (active) · PARTICIPLE (passive)
Rule 4have · has · had→ PARTICIPLE
Perception · 04 · Tense × aspect

Twelve tenses. One pattern.

Tense tells when. Aspect tells the shape of the action: complete, in progress, finished before a point, or lasting up to a point. Four aspects × three times = twelve — all built with the same chain.

Same verb, four shapes: I work · I am working · I have worked · I have been working
Perception · 05 · Verb types

Know what kind of verb you are using.

The same word can change type with context: “She looks tired” (linking) · “She looked at the map” (action) · “She looked up the word” (phrasal).

Integration · 07 · Voice and mood

Who acts? What do you intend?

Voice moves the focus between the doer and the receiver. Mood is the speaker’s intention: a fact, a command, or an unreal wish. These two controls make English sound mature.

Voice

Active vs. passive

Active: the subject does the action. Passive: the subject receives it — use it when the doer is unknown, obvious or less important.

Active: The chef cooks dinner.
Passive: Dinner is cooked by the chef.
Past passive: The bridge was built in 1990.
No doer needed: English is spoken here.
Formula: receiver + BE (any tense) + past participle (+ by + doer). Chain rule 3 again.
Mood

The speaker’s intention

Mood is not emotion. It is the grammatical intention behind the verb.

Indicative · fact: He goes to class.
Imperative · command: Go to class.
Negative command: Don't be late.
Subjunctive · unreal: I wish he were here.
Imperative: base verb, no subject. Subjunctive: limited but powerful in formal English — “If I were you…”, “I suggest that he be on time.”

Integration · 11 · Interference map

Eight traps Spanish sets for you.

Each of these is Spanish logic running inside an English verb phrase. Name the leak, hear the correct model, and the error loses its power.

Trap 01 · double marking

Only one word carries the ‑s.

×

She doesn't works here.

✓

She doesn't work here.

DOES already carries the ‑s and the time. Chain rule 1: do / does / did → base.

Trap 02 · “estoy de acuerdo”

Agree is a verb, not a state.

×

I am agree with you.

✓

I agree with you. I don't agree.

“Estar de acuerdo” has estar; English “agree” is one verb. No BE.

Trap 03 · modal + to

Modals never take “to”.

×

Can you to help me? You must to rest.

✓

Can you help me? You must rest.

Chain rule 2: modal → base verb. No to, no ‑s, no DO.

Trap 04 · “tengo 25 años”

Age, hunger and fear use BE.

×

I have 25 years. I have hungry.

✓

I am 25 (years old). I am hungry.

Spanish “tener” becomes English BE for age, hunger, thirst, cold, fear and being right.

Trap 05 · “quiero que vengas”

Want + person + to.

×

I want that you come.

✓

I want you to come.

want / need / ask / tell + object + to + base. “That” never follows want.

Trap 06 · “disfruto aprender”

Some verbs demand ‑ing.

×

I enjoy to learn. I avoid to speak.

✓

I enjoy learning. I avoid speaking.

enjoy, avoid, finish, keep, mind, suggest → ‑ing. The pattern belongs to the verb.

Trap 07 · “lo he terminado ayer”

Finished time kills the perfect.

×

I have finished it yesterday.

✓

I finished it yesterday. I have already finished.

yesterday, last week, in 2020 → past simple. The present perfect connects to now — no finished time.

Trap 08 · “vivo aquí desde 2019”

Since / for need the perfect.

×

I live here since 2019.

✓

I have lived here since 2019. I have been working here for five years.

Spanish uses the present for “desde / hace”. English measures from past to now → present perfect (simple or progressive).
