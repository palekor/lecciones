01 · The engine

Every sentence has a skeleton.

Students who memorize isolated sentences plateau fast. Students who own this five-piece engine can generate any present-tense sentence on demand — and, later, extend it with an adjective or a time clause without breaking the structure.

Subject chooses the actor. Verb + suffix carries the action. Complement finishes the thought.

This is the exact order used across the whole class: pronoun → verb → the small ending that agrees with it → the auxiliary that turns it into a question or negative → whatever finesse (adjective, time) you add at the end.

Step 1Subject

I / you / he / she / it / we / they

Step 2Verb

regular (walk) or irregular (go)

Step 3Suffix

-s / -es / -ing / -ed, or an irregular change

Step 4Auxiliary

do / does / did / am / is / are

Step 5Complement

adjective, time clause, place, object

Core rule: he / she / it always steals a suffix in present simple. Correct: She works from home. Incorrect: She work from home.
02 · Subject pronouns

Seven words. Every sentence starts here.

Every pronoun has a Spanish-vowel trap somewhere. Tune the phonetics up before speed. Remember: three of the seven are third person, "it" carries no gender, and "you" never changes between singular and plural.

03 · Sound before speed

The phoneme is the smallest unit.

A phoneme is the smallest piece of sound that still carries meaning. Change one, and the meaning changes with it. This class treats phonetics as a discipline, not decoration — trust the process even when English vowels have no Spanish equivalent.

You need two words to build a phrase — but you need clean sound to be understood at all.

Tap each word below to hear it. Notice how one sound swap changes the whole word: sun ≠ son, even though the spelling logic looks similar to a Spanish ear.

Try it: can you think of three more phonemes you already know? Say them out loud before moving on.
04 · Regular vs. irregular

Two families. One logic.

This step is only about affirmations and present tenses — speed and pattern recognition first, perfect accuracy later. Regular verbs never change their spine. Irregular verbs change shape between present and past, but still follow the same suffix logic in the present.

Regular verbs

Present stays the same. Past always adds -ed. Future keeps the base form after "will".

walk → walked → will walk

Irregular verbs

Present looks regular. Past changes shape completely and must be memorized one by one.

go → went → will go

Suffixes

Regular takes -s, -ing, -er, -ed. Irregular still takes -es, -ing in the present.

work → works / working

05 · Conjugation lab

Simple mode vs. progressive mode.

Simple mode is pronoun + verb, nothing else — that's the rule for this step. Progressive mode always needs two parts: am / is / are, plus the verb wearing -ing. Pick any verb from the bank and watch both modes build themselves across all seven pronouns.

08 · Finesse

Adjectives and time clauses.

The engine is solid. Now dress it. Adjectives describe the subject or object; time clauses tell the listener when. Both attach to the same skeleton you already built — nothing new to learn, only new furniture.

Adjectives sit in two places: after to be, or before the noun.

After a "be" verb, one adjective is enough: She is tired. I am happy. Before a noun, when you stack more than one adjective, follow this order so it sounds natural instead of translated:

1Opinion
2Size
3Age
4Shape
5Color
6Origin
7Material
Example: a beautiful (opinion) small (size) old (age) round (shape) brown (color) Colombian (origin) wooden (material) table. Elementary level only needs two or three of these at once.

Time clauses connect two actions.

A time clause tells the listener exactly when the main action happens. It attaches to a sentence you already know how to build — just add the connector.

When

A general or single moment.

When I finish my homework, I watch TV.
While

Two things happening at once — pairs well with progressive.

She is studying while I am cooking.
Before / After

Order of two actions.

Before you go, close the door.
As soon as

Immediate sequence.

I call her as soon as I arrive.

10 · Teacher guide

Run it as a live class.

A simple class route that keeps the page pedagogical, not just decorative.

Opening · 5 min

Ask: "Which pronoun has no gender?" Land on "it" — place, animal, or thing.

Phonemes · 5 min

Drill sun / son out loud. Confirm the class hears the difference before continuing.

Verb system + suffixes · 15 min

Contrast regular vs. irregular. Drill did vs. do/does until it's automatic.

Verb Bank + Conjugation Lab · 15 min

Every student picks one regular and one irregular verb and conjugates both modes live.

Builder + Quiz · 15 min

Use the Build Lab first, then the quiz. Name the broken part on any error: subject, verb, suffix, auxiliary, or complement.

Finesse close · 10 min

Each student adds one adjective and one time clause to a sentence they built earlier.
