01 · The construction rule

Three ideas. Two relationships.

A chunk is not a random chain of words. Each idea needs its own subject and verb, and each connector must accurately name the relationship between neighboring ideas.

Build each idea first. Connect the logic second.

If one part cannot communicate a message by itself, it is not yet a complete independent idea. A connector does not repair an incomplete structure—it explains how complete thoughts relate. A subordinating connector such as because makes its clause dependent, but that clause still keeps its own subject and finite verb.

02 · Before connecting

Make every idea complete.

A complete idea answers at least two questions: who or what is the message about, and what happens or is true?

01

Name the subject

The subject establishes the participant: she, they, the students, Prof. Germandario.

WHO? → The students
02

Choose the verb system

The verb carries time and viewpoint: practice, are practicing, practiced, will practice.

WHAT HAPPENS? → are practicing
03

Complete the message

Add the object, place, manner, or time needed to make the idea useful and specific.

WHAT / WHERE? → clearer sentences
03 · Relationship words

Connectors carry meaning.

Do not choose a connector only because it sounds familiar. Decide whether the next idea adds, contrasts, explains, results, sequences, or overlaps.

addition

and

Adds another idea of equal importance. The second idea continues or expands the first.

IDEA A + additional IDEA B

She studies daily, and she reviews at night.

Both habits are true and equally important.

They are learning, and they are enjoying the process.

Two current activities are added.
Punctuation: When and joins two complete ideas, a comma before and is clear and standard—especially when the ideas are long.
04 · Viewpoint

Simple or progressive?

Tense locates an event in time. Aspect shows how the speaker views it: as a whole, repeated fact or as an activity in progress around a reference moment.

SIMPLE VIEWPOINT

Present simple

See the action as a fact, whole event, routine, or completed unit.

subject + base verb / -s
She studies every other day.
  • routine or repeated habit
  • fact or stable condition
  • schedule or general truth
PROGRESSIVE VIEWPOINT

Present progressive

Look inside the activity and see it unfolding around a reference moment.

subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
She is studying right now.
  • action happening now
  • temporary activity around now
  • changing or developing situation
05 · Time × viewpoint

Six useful choices.

Simple and progressive are not better or worse versions of the same sentence. They answer different questions about frequency, completion, background, and duration.

Past
Past simplesubject + past verb

Completed event or finished sequence: "She studied yesterday."

Past progressivesubject + was/were + -ing

Activity in progress around a past moment: "She was studying at 7."

Present
Present simplesubject + base / -s

Routine, fact, permanent state: "She studies every other day."

Present progressivesubject + am/is/are + -ing

Activity happening now or temporary project: "She is studying now."

Future
Future simplesubject + will + base

Prediction, promise, decision, future fact: "She will study tomorrow."

Future progressivesubject + will be + -ing

Activity that will be in progress at a future moment: "She will be studying at 7."

06 · Worked example

Read the logic, not only the words.

The original message becomes clearer when spelling, connector relationships, aspect, and the program description are made precise.

She is learning how to create clearer sentences because she studies every other day with Prof. Germandario, and they are following a route-map program.

Idea 1 · current development

Present progressive presents learning as an active process happening around now.

Idea 2 · reason and routine

Because introduces the cause; present simple fits the repeated schedule "every other day."

Idea 3 · added current project

And adds another complete idea; present progressive presents the program as ongoing.

Language note: "Studying" has one d. "Every other day" means one day of study followed by one day without study. "Follow a route-map program" is more natural here than "go on a route map program."
