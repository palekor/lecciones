01 · The learning model

A sound is a coordinated movement.

Spanish and English organize their sound systems differently. When English requires a position absent from Spanish, the mouth initially returns to its strongest familiar pattern. That is motor transfer—not inability.

Hardware creates the phoneme. Software delivers it.

A listener identifies the sound from the physical configuration and acoustic result. Speaking louder, slower, or with a different pitch cannot repair the wrong tongue or lip position; first establish the target, then make it fluent.

LINGUISTIC HARDWAREBuild the sound

Lungs, vocal folds, pharynx, soft palate, jaw, tongue, alveolar ridge, teeth, and lips coordinate airflow, voicing, resonance, and contact.

THEN
SPEECH SOFTWAREShape the message

Volume controls intensity; speed controls timing; pitch carries intonation; stress and rhythm organize important and reduced information.

Important: an unfamiliar sound should feel unfamiliar. Awkward movement is evidence that the articulators are leaving a Spanish motor pattern. It is rehearsal—not failure.
02 · The linguistic apparatus

Know what you are moving.

Pronunciation uses four coordinated systems: breathing supplies energy, phonation adds voice, resonance shapes tone, and articulation converts airflow into recognizable consonants and vowels.

Lungs & diaphragm

Supply controlled outward air. More pressure can increase volume, but it does not determine the identity of a phoneme.

Vocal folds

Vibrate for voiced sounds such as /z/ and /b/; remain apart for voiceless partners such as /s/ and /p/. Their vibration rate influences pitch.

Velum

Raises to block the nose for oral sounds; lowers to send air through the nose for /m/, /n/, and /ŋ/.

Tongue & jaw

Control vowel height/frontness and most consonant contact. Tiny changes can change the word: ship /ʃɪp/ versus sheep /ʃiːp/.

Lips & teeth

Spread, relax, round, close, or create friction. English /θ/ and /ð/ require the tongue at the teeth—an unfamiliar Spanish setting for many learners.

03 · Confidence is part of technique

Break the false-failure loop.

When a sound does not exist in Spanish, perception and production may initially collapse it into the nearest Spanish category. Misunderstanding can then feel personal, even though the cause is trainable coordination.

01

Missing category

The English contrast is absent or organized differently in Spanish.

02

Old motor map

The mouth automatically selects its closest familiar position.

03

Sound ambiguity

The listener may hear another word or request repetition.

04

Self-blame

A physical mismatch is incorrectly interpreted as personal failure.

05

Reduced practice

Shyness limits repetitions, slowing the new motor map.

Reset the interpretation: “My hardware has not automated this position yet.” Practice slowly, exaggerate safely, compare two sounds, and accumulate successful repetitions. Confidence follows evidence from training.

05 · Sentence & shadowing builder

Hear. Follow. Repeat.

Select any of the 44 sounds and one of its two tailored sentences. Listen once, shadow slowly, then match the natural model without losing the mouth position.

SHADOWING LINE

Three-pass method: 1) listen without speaking; 2) repeat just behind the slow model; 3) shadow the natural model while preserving the target hardware setting.
06 · Mobile practice route

Short sessions build automatic movement.

Train two or three sounds per session. Quality falls when the mouth is tired; distributed physical practice is stronger than one long session.

PASS 01 · POSITION

Mirror × 5

Set lips, tongue, teeth, jaw, airflow, and voice without rushing.

PASS 02 · WORDS

Four words × 3

Tap each model, pause, and repeat while keeping the same sound identity.

PASS 03 · SENTENCES

Two lines × 3

Shadow slowly, then naturally. Keep stress and rhythm after placement is stable.

PASS 04 · CONTRAST

Review tomorrow

Return after sleep and alternate the sound with its nearest confusing neighbor.
