01 · The core idea

Meaning moves with the speaker.

A deictic expression cannot be fully understood without context. We need to know who is speaking, where the speaker is, and when the speaker says it.

Every message has an invisible “I · here · now” center.

From that center, English points to people, locations, moments, objects, and directions. If the center changes, the reference may change even when the words stay exactly the same.

●
Person

I / you depend on who is speaking and who is listening.

⌖
Space

Here / this / near are interpreted from a location.

◷
Time

Now / today / later are interpreted from a moment.

I need this package here now.

03 · Space and distance

Near the center or away from it?

English organizes spatial reference around proximity. The boundary is flexible: “here” may mean this chair, this room, this city, or even this country.

A flexible distance scale

The situation—not a fixed number of meters—defines “near” and “far.”

Near / nearby

Near can take an object: “near the station.” Nearby normally stands alone: “The station is nearby.”

Close to

Close needs to before an object: “close to my house.” Do not say “close my house” for distance.

Far from

Far normally uses from before the reference point: “far from here.”

04 · Time reference

Locate the moment.

The speaking moment is “now.” Other time words point backward or forward from it, so their calendar meaning changes every day.

beforeEARLIER · AGO

Three hours ago; earlier today.

yesterdayDAY BEFORE TODAY

The previous calendar day.

nowAT THIS MOMENT

The speaker’s current time.

laterAFTER NOW

A future moment, not precisely stated.

tomorrowDAY AFTER TODAY

The next calendar day.

Important: use ago after a completed amount of past time—“two days ago.” Use in before a future amount—“in two days.”
05 · Direction and viewpoint

Movement has a center too.

Come and bring normally move toward the speaker, listener, or chosen meeting point. Go and take normally move away from that center.

Come / bring

→ ●

Come moves a person toward the center. Bring moves an object toward the center with someone.

Please come here now.
Bring that chair over here.

Go / take

● →

Go moves a person away from the center. Take moves an object away from the center with someone.

Go there tomorrow.
Take this package over there.

This / these vs. that / those

✓

This book here is mine.One thing close to the center.

✓

Those books over there are yours.Multiple things away from the center.

Here / there as destinations

✓

Come here.Toward my current place.

✓

Go there.Toward the place I indicate away from me.

×

Go to there.Here and there normally do not take “to.”
